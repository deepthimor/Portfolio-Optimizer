## Chunk ID: finance-note-001

### Title: Diversification

### Source Filename

docs/finance-notes.md

### Note

Diversification means spreading a portfolio across different holdings, sectors, asset classes, or geographies instead of relying heavily on one investment.

A diversified portfolio may include stocks, ETFs, bonds, cash, and international exposure. The goal is to reduce the impact of one holding or sector performing poorly.

### Simple Example

If a portfolio is 90% one technology stock, the portfolio depends heavily on that company. If the stock falls sharply, the whole portfolio may fall sharply.

If the portfolio is spread across several asset classes and sectors, one holding has less control over the total result.

### Limitations

Diversification does not eliminate risk. In broad market downturns, many asset classes can fall at the same time.

## Chunk ID: finance-note-002

### Title: Volatility

### Source Filename

docs/finance-notes.md

### Note

Volatility describes how much an investment or portfolio moves up and down over time.

A highly volatile portfolio may have large gains in some periods and large losses in others. A less volatile portfolio may move more gradually.

### Simple Example

A portfolio of early-stage technology stocks may move more than a portfolio holding broad-market ETFs, bonds, and cash.

### Limitations

Volatility is not the same as permanent loss. Some volatile assets recover, while some less volatile assets can still lose value.

## Chunk ID: finance-note-003

### Title: Concentration

### Source Filename

docs/finance-notes.md

### Note

Concentration means a large portion of a portfolio is tied to one holding, sector, asset class, or theme.

High concentration can increase upside if the concentrated position performs well, but it can also increase downside if that position performs poorly.

### Simple Example

If one stock is 50% of a portfolio, that stock has a large influence on the portfolio’s total value.

If that stock falls 30%, the total portfolio may experience a meaningful decline even if the other holdings are stable.

### Limitations

Concentration is not always bad. Some investors intentionally concentrate when they have strong conviction. However, it increases dependence on fewer outcomes.

## Chunk ID: finance-note-004

### Title: Rebalancing

### Source Filename

docs/finance-notes.md

### Note

Rebalancing means adjusting a portfolio back toward a target allocation.

Over time, some holdings may grow faster than others. This can cause the portfolio to drift away from the investor’s intended risk level.

### Simple Example

A portfolio starts with 60% stocks and 40% bonds. After stocks rise, the portfolio becomes 75% stocks and 25% bonds.

Rebalancing could involve reducing stock exposure or adding to bonds so the portfolio moves closer to the original target.

### Limitations

Rebalancing can involve taxes, transaction costs, timing risk, and personal financial considerations. This project does not account for those factors.

## Chunk ID: finance-note-005

### Title: Cash Drag

### Source Filename

docs/finance-notes.md

### Note

Cash drag happens when a portfolio holds a large amount of cash and the invested assets rise faster than cash.

Cash can reduce volatility and provide flexibility, but too much cash may lower long-term return if markets rise.

### Simple Example

If a portfolio is 50% cash and the stock market rises 20%, only the invested portion benefits from that market increase.

### Limitations

Cash is not always a drag. It can be useful for emergency needs, near-term expenses, lower-risk preferences, or waiting for investment opportunities.

## Chunk ID: finance-note-006

### Title: Sequence Risk

### Source Filename

docs/finance-notes.md

### Note

Sequence risk is the risk that poor returns happen at an especially bad time.

This matters most when someone is withdrawing money from a portfolio. Losses early in a withdrawal period can have a larger long-term impact than losses later.

### Simple Example

Two portfolios may have the same average return over time, but the one that suffers large losses early while withdrawals are happening may run down faster.

### Limitations

Sequence risk depends on withdrawals, time horizon, spending needs, asset allocation, and market conditions. This project only explains the concept.

## Chunk ID: finance-note-007

### Title: Risk Tolerance

### Source Filename

docs/finance-notes.md

### Note

Risk tolerance describes how much uncertainty, volatility, or potential loss an investor is willing and able to accept.

A conservative investor may prefer more cash and bonds. An aggressive investor may accept more stock exposure and higher volatility.

### Simple Example

Two investors with the same portfolio size may choose different allocations because one values stability while the other is comfortable with larger swings.

### Limitations

Risk tolerance is personal. It can depend on age, income, goals, time horizon, financial obligations, and emotional comfort with losses.
